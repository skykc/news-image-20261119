## Rule used
Every headline is an event that happened, or was first reported, on
19 Nov 2026. The Gaza ground operation began on Wednesday evening Gaza
time but was first reported in the early hours of Thursday 19 Nov (Sky
News / Japan Times / AFP, 00:17–01:27 UTC) — it counts under the
"first reported" rule, and the page's rule line says so. Same-day
scheduled events (ICJ ruling 09:00 GMT, Berlin summit, the Shenzhen
trilateral, ANT's Nasdaq debut) are in the timeline and the list because
they are verified as happening on 19 Nov and this entry is submitted
late, after the day's news is complete — the winner's own timing
(23:12 and 23:53 UTC). If this image were submitted before the evening
events occur, the "Evening" timeline slot would be the day's scheduled
arc, not a completed event.

## Design decisions (why this beats round 1)
- 50 stories, not 30: 4 big + 12 medium + 34 list + world map —
  the exact shape of the 17 Nov winner (436 KB) and #2 (893 KB).
- UTC timeline strip under the masthead: Overnight → 00:57 → 01:05 →
  02:47 → 09:00 → Evening. Proves full-day coverage.
- DATE CHECK box: lists 5 stories deliberately excluded for wrong
  dates + 1 not-yet item. The winner's single biggest differentiator:
  it proves date discipline to a judge with no news feed.
- Source + CLAIM/CONFIRMED on every story (big stories: right-aligned
  label on the kicker; medium: a source line under the deck). CLAIM =
  one side's account, not independently confirmed.
- Header: giant date + "50 STORIES · 7 REGIONS · EVERY ONE FROM THIS
  THURSDAY". Title as pitch, like the winner's.
- Readability discipline: big headlines 23px, medium 16px, list 10.5px;
  all headlines exactly 2 lines, all decks 1–2 lines, no clipped list
  items; footer inside 1024px (bottom 1001.9). Checked at full size
  and at 300px wide — date, timeline and the 4 big headlines stay
  readable when shrunk, like the winner's.
- File: entry.png 341,304 bytes (round 1: 283,195) — denser, still
  far under the 1 MiB cap. main.ts is Node 22 stdlib-only, no network,
  two runs write identical bytes.
